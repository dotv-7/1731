From Low Tide #1 (14-03-2024), repeated in #6, #9 and #12:

Line one — the "as of" date inside the document, not the publication date. Subtract it from today: 12 days is normal, 25 means someone chose not to publish on time, 40 means stop reading and go look at the chain. An attestation is a snapshot of one moment, so the gap tells you whether the snapshot is of the moment the issuer claims.

Line two — the custodian signature. A custodian named inside the issuer's own PDF is a claim; a letter on the custodian's letterhead confirming the balance is a counterparty putting its name on that claim. Spelling of the custodian's name counts, because a misspelling means someone retyped the document.

Line three — the liability line, the one almost nobody reads. Reserves are the asset side, and reserves without liabilities is theatre: a photo of a full vault with no mention of how many people hold a key.

Why those three: each one tests something the headline ratio cannot fake — whether the numbers are current, whether a second party stands behind them, and whether the tokens those assets back are actually counted. Everything else in the PDF is decoration until those hold.

As Salt puts it in #1: the three lines catch sloppiness and omission, not a competent liar. For that you need the chain.
